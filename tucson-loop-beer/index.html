Where Can You Grab a Beer Along The Loop?

The Chuck Huckelberry Loop is Tucson’s network of shared-use paths along the rivers and washes. This map shows every brewery, bar and restaurant licensed to pour a beer within a short bike ride of it, and the way off the path to each one.

The ride is measured over the real street and path network from the nearest point where you can actually leave the Loop, so a bar just over the fence from the path can still be a long way round. It builds on the network from Tucson Loop Access.

    These are places you could nearly throw a bottle cap at from the path, where the legal way round is several times longer. The detour to Catalina Brewing is real: there is no direct way in from the path. For the others, the cause may be a gate or a short link that the street map does not have.

    Data sources and methods

    • Places: active Arizona liquor licences in Pima County, from the City of Tucson’s public “Liquor License - Active” map layer, which copies the Arizona Department of Liquor Licenses and Control’s list every day. Downloaded …. Only licences to pour on the premises are used: microbrewery, bar, beer and wine bar, restaurant, and hotel. Shops, private clubs and one-off permits are left out. The data is provided “as is”.
    • What a licence does not tell you: whether the taps are any good, the opening hours, or whether the doors are open today. A licence can stay active after a place shuts. A beer and wine bar licence is also held by some pizza places, a barber or two, and a grocery store.
    • Kinds: a brewery holds a microbrewery licence or has “brewing”, “brewery” or “cider house” in its name. Hotels, golf clubs, cinemas and similar venues are picked out by name. Everything else goes by its licence.
    • Locations: the City’s geocoding of each licence address, which it calls approximate outside the city limits. About one in a hundred was well off. … were moved, most to the OpenStreetMap place of the same name. Some wrong ones surely remain.
    • Names: the licence list is in capitals. Where OpenStreetMap has the same place, its spelling is used. Otherwise the name is put in title case by rule, so a few read oddly.
    • Ride: the fastest legal route from the Loop to the nearest street corner to each place, at … mph, on the network from Tucson Loop Access. One-way streets are one-way and private streets cannot be cut through. Traffic is not counted: a mile on a fast arterial costs the same as a mile on a quiet street.
    • The Loop: its extent is assembled from OpenStreetMap, because no single route there covers all of it, so a few pieces may be missing or extra. Parts of the Loop are named after the river or wash they follow.
    • Streets, paths and names: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.
