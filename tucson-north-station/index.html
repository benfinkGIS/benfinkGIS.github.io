Where Could the Train Stop North of Tucson?

The Arizona Department of Transportation is studying passenger trains between Phoenix and Tucson on the Union Pacific line. Its Service Development Plan, due in two to three years, is to settle how many stations the route gets and where they go. Nothing is funded to build yet.

Tucson already has a depot downtown. This page asks where a second, northern station could go: it takes every piece of open land beside the track from … rail miles out to the Pinal County line, … miles out, drops the ones a station would not fit on, and counts who could drive to the rest.

    Who each site would bring within reach

     

    The bars count Pima County residents who could drive to the site within the limit and could not drive to the downtown depot in that time. That is the case for a second station: people the first one leaves out. A site close to town reaches more people in all, but most of them already have the depot.

    The sites that pass

    Passing the screen means a station would physically fit and a street reaches the land. It does not mean the owner would sell, the town would rezone it, or Union Pacific would agree to a platform there. … of the … sites are more than half inside a mapped 1% flood zone, which a station can be built in but has to be raised for.

    What ruled the others out

    … pieces of open land touch the railroad’s right-of-way. … of them fail at least one test.

    Data sources and methods

    • The line: the Union Pacific main line from Pima County’s railroad layer. Rail miles are measured along it from the downtown depot on Toole Avenue. The county line is … miles out; land in Pinal County (Red Rock and beyond) is not screened.
    • Land: Pima County’s regional parcel layer, … parcels within … ft of the track. A parcel is open if the assessor’s use code says vacant, farm or ranch, or publicly owned. Parcels with the interstate or a frontage road running through them are treated as highway right-of-way. Touching open parcels with the same owner are joined into one site.
    • Beside the track: the site has to come within … ft of Union Pacific’s own land, along at least … ft of straight track, enough for a platform.
    • Room: at least … acres that are outside the regulatory floodway and at least … ft across, for a platform, a small building, a bus loop and a few hundred parking spaces. Building footprints may cover no more than … of the site.
    • Flood: Pima County Regional Flood Control District floodways, and FEMA’s 1% annual chance zones (A, AE, AH, AO). Floodway land does not count as room. The flood zone share is reported, not used to rule a site out.
    • A way in: the nearest county street that a driveway could reach without crossing the track or Interstate 10. A site with none is ruled out, even if a farm crossing exists.
    • Drive times: Pima County’s street network, …. No traffic and no signal delay, so trips on surface streets come out a few minutes fast. Each 2020 Census block is placed at its internal point and joined to the nearest street junction.
    • Who is counted: residents of Pima County only (… in 2020). People in SaddleBrooke, Red Rock and the rest of Pinal County would use a northern station too and are not in these numbers. Jobs, bus connections and time saved on the train itself are not counted either.
    • Not checked: who would sell, zoning changes, utilities, drainage design, environmental review, and Union Pacific’s own track plans. The drive areas on the map are rough outlines.
    • Basemap: Esri. Script: tools/build_north_station.py in the site’s repository.
