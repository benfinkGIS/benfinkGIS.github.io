How to label

Photos are from autumn 2023. The yellow ticks point at the spot; nothing is drawn on it. If the photo does not settle it, the Google Maps button opens the same spot, where Street View usually does.

Crossings: how does the wash get past the road?

  • Bridge: the road is carried over on a deck, open underneath. Count a wide box culvert you could walk through upright as a bridge.
  • Culvert: the road sits on fill and the wash goes through a pipe or low box. Headwalls, a pipe mouth, or a channel that stops at one road edge and restarts at the other.
  • At-grade: the road dips through the wash bed. Sand or stain across the pavement, “Do not enter when flooded” country.
  • No crossing here: there is no wash at this spot, or it runs alongside the road without crossing.

Channel beds: what is the channel made of at the mark?

  • Natural: sand, soil, rock or plants on the bed, and banks nobody has built.
  • Natural bed, built banks: an earthen or sandy bed between soil cement, riprap, gabions or walls.
  • Concrete: a poured or paved floor.
  • Not a wash: a street, parking lot, yard or roof drain. Nothing you would call a channel.
  • If the mark lands on a bridge deck or a road over the wash, label the channel as it is on either side, since that is what runs underneath. If the two sides differ, or you cannot see either, choose Can't tell.
  • If you honestly cannot say, choose Can't tell. Those are counted and reported, not guessed.
  • Keys: 1 to 4 for a class, 0 for can't tell, H to hide the marks, ← → to move. A label saves at once and moves to the next unlabelled item.
