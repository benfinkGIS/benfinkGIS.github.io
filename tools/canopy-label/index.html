How to label
  • Label only what is inside the small yellow square at the centre (half a metre across), as seen from above in the October 2023 photo.
  • Tree canopy: the crown of a tree or tall cactus, roughly taller than a person can reach (2.5 m).
  • Roof: any building or carport roof.
  • Pavement: asphalt, concrete, sidewalks, driveways, patios, sport courts.
  • Low vegetation: grass, shrubs, hedges, weeds, small cactus. Living plants below tree height.
  • Bare ground: soil, gravel, rock mulch, dirt alleys and lots.
  • Go by the surface in the photo, not what the place is used for. A sports field or lawn that shows red in the infrared view is Low vegetation; one that is dry or dormant (tan, and not red in infrared) is Bare ground.
  • Water: pools and ponds.
  • On an edge between two surfaces: label whichever fills more of the yellow square. Judge it from the photo as it is; do not shift the point to where you think the edge "should" be. If it is an even split you cannot call, choose Can't tell.
  • A vehicle, bin or other object: label the surface it is sitting on.
  • A shadow: label the surface the shadow falls on, using the wide view to judge.
  • If you honestly cannot say, choose Can't tell. Those points are counted and reported, not guessed.
  • Keys: 1 to 6 for a class, 0 for can't tell, ← → to move. A label saves at once and moves to the next unlabelled point.
