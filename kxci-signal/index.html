Who Can Hear KXCI?

KXCI 91.3 FM is Tucson’s community radio station. Its licence allows … watts from an antenna … metres up a tower southeast of the city, a few miles north of Corona de Tucson.

The FCC draws every FM station’s service area as a single line, the 60 dBu contour. It works that line out from the station’s power, the height of its antenna, and the average height of the ground 3 to 16 km out in each direction. A ridge 30 km away does not come into it. Tucson has four mountain ranges in the way, so this page runs a terrain model over the same ground and counts who the line misses.

    Three ways to count the audience

    Residents at or above 60 dBu, out of … who live within … km of the tower.

    At rooftop height the two predictions nearly agree on the total and disagree on the places. The terrain model drops … residents inside the line, and adds … outside it, most of them in Marana, down the Santa Cruz valley where nothing stands in the way. Lower the antenna to car height and … residents inside the line fall under 60 dBu.

    60 dBu is the level the FCC protects from interference. It is not the edge of hearing: a decent car radio keeps playing well below it. Read the shadows as “weaker than the map promises”, not as silence.

    Town by town

    Residents at or above 60 dBu in each town of … people or more within … km. Largest first.

    Oro Valley is the largest gap. The FCC’s line takes in … of its … residents, and the terrain model puts … of those under 60 dBu: the town sits behind the west end of the Santa Catalinas as seen from the tower. On the west side, in the Tucson Mountains foothills and Valencia West, most residents clear 60 dBu at rooftop height and most do not at car height.

    Ask for KXCI, get 1991

    The FCC has a public service that returns a station’s contour by call sign. Ask it for KXCI and it answers with a licence from 1991: … watts on Mt. Bigelow in the Catalinas, … km from where the station’s licence puts it now. The FCC’s own licence database shows the present site, licensed on 4 April 2024. The contour on this page is computed from that record. The old one is on the map as a dashed line (switch it on under Settings).

    Data sources and methods

    • Licence: the FCC’s FM Query (licence file …, facility …): class …, … watts, non-directional, antenna centre … m above sea level. The elevation data puts the ground at the tower … m below that, which matches the licence. The station also holds a licence for a backup transmitter in central Tucson, shown on the map and not modelled.
    • FCC contour: the 60 dBu F(50,50) contour from the FCC’s Contours API, computed from the licence figures along 360 radials with 1 arc-second terrain. It runs … to … km from the tower and covers … square km. It is a prediction, not a measurement.
    • People: 2020 Census blocks: residents, and occupied housing units as households (PL 94-171 table H1). A block wholly inside the contour counts in full, and a block the contour cuts counts by the share of its area inside.
    • Terrain model: the Irregular Terrain Model (Longley-Rice) in point-to-point mode, run with the itmlogic Python package over USGS 3DEP elevation at 30 m. 720 radials from the tower, a terrain profile every 100 m, a receiver every 200 m out to … km. Median in time, place and situation, to line up with the FCC’s F(50,50). Each block takes the value at its internal point.
    • Receiver heights: 9.1 m is the rooftop antenna the FCC’s curves assume, so it is the fair comparison with the contour. 1.5 m is a car aerial or a radio on a table.
    • What the model leaves out: buildings, trees, reflections, interference from other stations, and the vertical pattern of the antenna. The ground is bare earth with average soil. The model flags … of its own points, mostly far ones behind mountains, as outside the terrain geometry it was built on, so treat the edges of the shadows as soft.
    • Measurements: there are none on this page. Both maps are predictions.
    • Basemap: Esri. Scripts: tools/build_kxci.py, model_kxci_terrain.py and publish_kxci.py in the site’s repository.
