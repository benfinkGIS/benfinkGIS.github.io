Where Do You Release a Rescued Kit Fox?

The scores are judgment calls, and nothing here has been checked on the ground.

A wildlife rehabilitation center in central Arizona rescued and rehabilitated a kit fox and needed somewhere to release it. This project scores the land within 100 miles of the center for how well it suits a kit fox, then narrows the result to a short list of areas worth a closer look.

The model weighs eight things, from vegetation and soil to how far the nearest town is. Step through them below, then change the weights yourself to see how much the answer depends on them.

Data sources and methods

  • The model: a weighted overlay built in ArcGIS Pro’s Suitability Modeler. Each criterion is scored from 1 to 10 on a 30 m grid, and the final score is the weighted average. The weights add up to 100. The search area is a 100-mile circle around the center, … km².
  • Vegetation type and cover: LANDFIRE 2024 Existing Vegetation Type and Existing Vegetation Cover.
  • Soils: USDA NRCS SSURGO soil map units.
  • Night lights: VIIRS Nighttime Lights, 2024 annual composite.
  • Elevation and slope: USGS 30 m elevation model.
  • Development and farmland: the developed and agricultural classes of LANDFIRE 2024, and straight-line distance from them.
  • Water: streams, washes and water bodies from the USGS National Hydrography Dataset, and straight-line distance from them. In this desert most of the mapped streams are washes that run only after rain.
  • The shortlist: every possible … km circle was given its average score. A circle stays in only if at least … of it is open public land, its average slope is …° or less, and no primary or secondary road comes within … km of its edge. The best … were then picked so that no two are closer than … km. Those limits are first guesses.
  • Land managers: Bureau of Land Management Arizona, Surface Management Agency, with the Arizona State Land Department and ALRIS. “Open public land” here means everything except private, tribal, military and other restricted federal land.
  • Designations: wilderness from the U.S. Forest Service and the Bureau of Land Management, and the BLM’s national monuments, national conservation areas and areas of critical environmental concern. Wilderness managed by the National Park Service or the Fish and Wildlife Service is not in these layers. A shortlisted area is tied to a named place only when it lies mostly inside it.
  • Roads: U.S. Census Bureau TIGER/Line 2023 primary and secondary roads. Roads are used only to screen the shortlist. They are not one of the model’s criteria.
  • What this cannot tell you: whether kit foxes already live in a place, whether there is prey, or whether a release there would be allowed. The scores have not been tested against kit fox sightings. A release needs the agreement of whoever manages the land and of the wildlife agency, and a visit first.
  • Map: scores on Esri’s light gray basemap. The weight sliders work on the 500 m cells, so the share of land scoring … or more reads a little lower there than on the full 30 m model (… km²).
